Benchmarks

How fenecdb compares with SQLite, PostgreSQL, MongoDB and Redis as a database first, then at search, measured by make targets you can run yourself.

What is measured

A page for each family, each with its tables and, collapsed under them, the method.

  • YCSB benchmarks

    Everyday reads and writes: YCSB's six core workloads over a million records. fenecdb in process against SQLite; fenec-server over HTTP against PostgreSQL, MongoDB and Redis. Durable and buffered, every cell, the ones fenecdb loses too.

  • Concurrency and request benchmarks

    Many writers and the readers beside them against SQLite, what one request to fenec-server costs and where its time goes, and what loading rows costs against PostgreSQL with pgvector.

  • Text search benchmarks

    What a search box costs (a match ranked by BM25, its marks, facets, and the words a typo reaches), and how good the answers are on two BEIR datasets.

  • Vector search benchmarks

    Search by meaning against SQLite and pgvector in process, fenec-server against pgvector at scale and at a million vectors, the ef and recall trade-off, and half precision.

  • Size and memory benchmarks

    What the browser downloads, how large each build is, what each feature adds to the WebAssembly module, and how the memory fenecdb counts compares with what the process uses.

How to read the tables

Every number here comes from crates/fenec-bench and can be reproduced with a make target. The method is written out beside each table, because a number without one is a claim, not a measurement.

  • A YCSB figure is the median of three runs, in thousands of operations a second, with the p99 of the workload's slowest operation under it. The other pages say what their figures are: the median of several runs, or one run after idle minutes.
  • Durable and buffered are the two settings the engines are compared in. Durable is an fsync a write, in each engine's own terms; buffered is each engine's default.
  • In process or over the wire. In process, fenecdb and SQLite are libraries in the benchmark's own program. Over the wire, fenec-server, PostgreSQL, MongoDB and Redis are each asked by a client, and the network is part of every figure: the YCSB page measures it, and compares the servers inside Docker for that reason.
  • One laptop, and idle minutes between runs. The timings are from an Apple M1 laptop, which is fanless and slows under minutes of load on every core, so a run starts once the machine is idle again and the systems take turns.
